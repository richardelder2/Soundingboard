# Grimdark & Mercenary Fantasy Questionnaire

## STAGE 1: THE MUD, THE SANGUINE & MORAL EXHAUSTION (World Tier)
1. Welcome the author to the mercenary muster camp. Ask if they are ready to forge a fantasy world stripped of shining armor and noble destiny, where steel bites, wounds fester, and survival is the only true victory.
2. "What is this bleak theater of war? Describe the weather, the landscape, and the ruin left by years of grinding conflict (e.g., knee-deep freezing trench mud, smoke hanging over burned wheat fields, carrion birds circling shattered siege engines)."
3. "What are the tactile sensory realities of soldiering here? (The smell of wet boiled leather, infected gangrene, rust on cheap iron blades, rancid salted pork, the dull ache of unhealed fractures)."
4. "The Subversion of Nobility: How does the nobility or high command view the frontline soldiers? (e.g., aristocratic officers dining on fine wine in heated silk pavilions while selling the mercenary company into a doomed slaughter for political coin)."

## STAGE 2: THE COMPANY, THE CODE & THE TROPE STACK
5. "Let's define your Trope Stack: **[dynamic] + [situation] + [flavor]** (e.g., *Doomed Mercenary Company + Impossible Contract + Bitter Gallows Humor*, or *Ex-Inquisitor Anti-Hero + Corrupt Holy War + Reluctant Protection Contract*). What stack are we bleeding for?"
6. "Introduce the Mercenary Company / Crew: What is their name, their banner or brand, and what notorious reputation precedes them across the taverns and battlefields?"
7. "The Pragmatic Code: In a world without chivalric honor, what unwritten mercenary code keeps these killers from slitting each other's throats while they sleep?"

## STAGE 3: THE ANTI-HERO & THE SQUAD CHEMISTRY
8. "Who is our lead Anti-Hero? (e.g., the scarred, cynical sergeant who just wants his men to live through the winter; the razor-sharp torturer who hates his job; or the disgraced noble fighting under a fake name)."
9. "What is their moral compromise? What unforgivable atrocity or cowardice from their past haunts them, and how have they rationalized it away?"

## STAGE 4: THE SUICIDAL CONTRACT & THE FALSE GODS
11. "The Contract: Who hires the company in Act 1, and what seems deceptively simple about the objective? (e.g., hold a remote mountain watchtower for three weeks; escort an arrogant inquisitor into a plague-ridden swamp)."
12. "The Trap / Betrayal: When do they realize they were never meant to survive the contract? Who sold them out, and what leverage forces them to keep marching forward?"
13. "The Low Magic & Eldritch Corruption: Magic in grimdark is neither clean nor benevolent. How does sorcery manifest here? What horrific price or bodily mutilation does a practitioner pay to wield it?"

## STAGE 5: THE SIEGE, THE ATTRITION & THE BITTER STAND
14. "The Midpoint Meatgrinder: Describe the brutal skirmish or failed retreat at 50% where half the squad is cut down, resources are lost, and medical supplies run dry."
15. "The Crisis of Pragmatism (75-80%): The lead is presented with an escape route—they can save their own skin if they abandon their wounded companions or butcher innocent villagers. What choice defines their twisted soul?"
16. "The Bloody Climax: How does the company execute their counter-ambush? In grimdark, victory is measured in surviving the day, killing the paymaster who sold them out, and leaving the battlefield on bloody feet."

## STAGE 6: THE SHAPE OF THE TELLING

17. "What narrative perspective and tone do you want? (Close, visceral third-person with dark gallows banter, visceral combat choreography, and psychological trauma; avoiding glorification of violence)."
18. "How will you handle character mortality? (Grimdark requires stakes where beloved squad members can die suddenly and unglamorously, impacting the survivors)."
19. "The Ending Note: Does the story end on cynical resignation, or a tiny, hard-won ember of human warmth in a cold, indifferent universe?"
