# Upmarket & Book Club Fiction Questionnaire

## STAGE 1: THE CURATED COMMUNITY & SOCIAL FAULT LINES (World Tier)
1. Welcome the author to the book club circle. Ask if they are ready to craft an emotionally gripping, psychologically acute novel that readers will argue over late into the night.
2. "What is our Setting & Social Milieu? Describe the tight-knit community and its unwritten social rules (e.g., an affluent coastal California enclave obsessed with academic prestige; a quiet Midwestern college town with simmering class friction; or a sprawling summer lake house community)."
3. "The Sensory Details of Modern Upmarket Life: Describe the physical atmosphere (The clink of chilled rosé glasses on limestone patios, the low murmur of school parking lot conversations, curated living rooms with neutral linens concealing deep debt or private despair)."
4. "The Surface Agreement / The Collective Lie: What polite illusion or moral fiction does this community agree to maintain to keep their social ecosystem intact?"

## STAGE 2: THE MORAL DILEMMA & THE TROPE STACK
5. "Let's define your Trope Stack: **[dynamic] + [situation] + [flavor]** (e.g., *Moral Dilemma with No Clean Answer + Domestic Secret + Community Backlash*, or *Estranged Sisters Reunion + Inheritance Scandal + Unreliable Memories*). What conversation are we starting?"
6. "The Core Moral Crossroads: What impossible choice sits at the center of this story? (A choice where every option carries real harm and profound ethical justification—the kind that makes book clubs fiercely debate who was right)."
7. "Who is our Protagonist? What is her outward competence (career, motherhood, artistic stature), and what private regret or compromise is eating away at her peace of mind?"

## STAGE 3: THE CATALYST & THE FRACTURE
8. "The Catalyst Event: What single incident in Chapter 1 detonates the curated surface of their lives? (e.g., an anonymous accusation at a school board meeting; a sudden medical discovery that questions paternity; or an accidental tragedy at a teenage party)."
9. "The Secret Kept: What lie or omission does the protagonist commit in the immediate aftermath to protect someone she loves?"
10. "The Domino Effect: How does that single lie begin pulling neighboring families, marriages, and friendships into the blast radius?"

## STAGE 4: THE INTERPERSONAL WEB & COMPETING TRUTHS
11. "The Foil / Antagonist with a Point: Who is the person challenging our protagonist? (Crucial: In upmarket fiction, the antagonist must NOT be an evil villain—they must be a person whose grievance is entirely understandable and sympathetic)."
12. "The Confidant Whose Loyalty is Tested: Who is the friend, spouse, or sibling caught in the middle, and what pressure threatens to break their silence?"
13. "The Generational Perspective: How do the children or adolescents view the adult drama? What do they see that the parents are willfully blind to?"

## STAGE 5: THE UNRAVELING, THE RECKONING & THE COST
14. "The Midpoint Public Rupture (50%): Where does the private secret burst into the public sphere? (At a dinner party, a school assembly, a local newspaper article, or a viral neighborhood forum thread?)."
15. "The Dark Moment (75-80%): When all rationalizations collapse, the marriage fractures, and the protagonist must look in the mirror and confront the truth of who they have become, what do they see?"
16. "The Climax / The Reckoning: Not all battles are fought with fists—how does the protagonist face the truth, apologize, or make a sacrifice that can never undo the damage, but allows healing to begin?"

## STAGE 6: THE SHAPE OF THE TELLING
These answers set the structural authenticity dials in `_config/narrative_authenticity.md` and feed Stage 02's `structure_plan.md`.
17. "What narrative structure and POV style best serves the emotional depth? (Single intimate first-person; rotating multi-POV between three women; or a non-linear 'Before and After' timeline tracking the countdown to the catalyst event?)."
18. "How will you handle psychological interiority and voice? (Layered, observant, beautifully crafted sentences that capture the quiet humor, sharp social observations, and raw vulnerabilities of modern life)."
19. "The Ending & Discussion Question: How does the book end? (Upmarket fiction favors bittersweet, earned emotional resolutions over tidy Hollywood bows—what is the central question the author wants readers debating?)."

## STAGE 7: SYNTHESIS
20. Review all answers. Synthesize them into an irresistible, provocative, and deeply moving upmarket fiction blueprint packed with emotional truth, sharp dialogue, and profound discussion potential. Present back for approval.
21. Once approved, craft a 12-word pitch and a compelling book club commercial blurb hook.
