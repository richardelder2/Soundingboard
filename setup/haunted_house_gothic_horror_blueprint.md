# Haunted House & Gothic Horror Questionnaire

## STAGE 1: THE ESTATE, THE ROT & MALEVOLENT ARCHITECTURE (World Tier)
1. Welcome the author to the threshold of the manor. Ask if they are ready to construct a claustrophobic gothic horror tale where the house itself is hungry, the walls keep bloody secrets, and sanity is the first thing that rots.
2. "What is our Manor / Estate? Describe its architectural style, remote geography, and history (e.g., a decaying Victorian gothic mansion on an eroding cliff in Yorkshire; a sprawling, humid antebellum plantation in the Louisiana swamps; or a Brutalist concrete mansion on an isolated island)."
3. "The Malevolent Anatomy of the House: What architectural quirks make the house feel alive or wrong? (Doors that don't stay closed, angles that seem slightly off 90 degrees, cold spots that drift between rooms, pipes that scream like children, black mold spreading in geometric patterns)."
4. "The Sensory Viscosity of Dread: Describe the sensory landscape (The smell of damp damp-rot, stagnant cellar water, and dead flowers; the scraping sound inside the walls at 3:00 AM; the suffocating weight of portraits whose painted eyes follow every movement)."

## STAGE 2: THE INHABITANTS, THE ARRIVAL & THE TROPE STACK
5. "Let's define your Trope Stack: **[dynamic] + [situation] + [flavor]** (e.g., *Inherited Ruined Estate + Generational Curse + Malevolent Architecture*, or *Grieving Family + Remote Renovation Project + Psychological Possession*). What nightmare are we building?"
6. "Who is our protagonist? (e.g., a grieving widow seeking solitude; an estranged heir arriving to appraise an eccentric ancestor's estate; or an architect hired to restore a historical structure with a gruesome past)."
7. "What internal vulnerability or grief do they bring into the house? (In great gothic fiction, the haunting feeds on preexisting guilt, trauma, or repressed memory)."

## STAGE 3: THE PAST SINS & WRONGNESS #1
8. "The Generational Sin: What horrific event occurred in this house decades or centuries ago? (An institutional cover-up, a family butchery, a hidden child starved in the attic, or an occult experiment conducted in the cellar?)."
9. "Wrongness #1: What is the first deniable supernatural manifestation? (e.g., finding antique dolls arranged in a circle at the foot of the bed; a scent of burning hair in a cold library; or footsteps pacing the floorboards above an empty room)."
10. "The Rationalization Phase: How does our protagonist try to explain it away for the first five chapters? (Drafts, plumbing, exhaustion, medication, or house-settling?)."

## STAGE 4: THE ISOLATION & THE ACCELERATING MANIFESTATIONS
11. "The Physical Isolation / The Snapped Line: What event cuts off escape from the outside world? (A bridge washed out by floodwaters, a blinding blizzard, or a car engine whose spark plugs have vanished)."
12. "The Local Folklore / The Warning: Who in the nearby village warns them to leave, and what terrifying nursery rhyme or superstition do the locals repeat about the house?"
13. "The Possession / Unreliable Perception: When do the house's manifestations begin directly attacking the protagonist's mind? (Hearing voices in the radio static, losing hours of memory, waking up with muddy shoes in bed, seeing loved ones with distorted faces)."

## STAGE 5: THE CELLAR, THE REVELATION & THE SIEGE
14. "The Midpoint Discovery (50%): What hidden room, sealed brick wall, or forgotten diary is uncovered at the halfway mark, proving without a doubt that the evil is real, deliberate, and cannot be appeased?"
15. "The Dark Moment (75-80%): When the house closes its jaws—locks turning from the inside, corridors rearranging themselves, reflections stepping out of mirrors—how is the protagonist stripped of all hope?"
16. "The Climax / The Pyre or Escape: How does the survivor fight back? In gothic horror, can the house ever truly be destroyed, or does escaping it require burning it to the ground and carrying the curse forever in one's mind?"

## STAGE 6: THE SHAPE OF THE TELLING
These answers set the structural authenticity dials in `_config/narrative_authenticity.md` and feed Stage 02's `structure_plan.md`.
17. "What narrative cadence and atmospheric style will you use? (Intimate, poetic, suffocating prose inspired by Shirley Jackson, Sarah Waters, and Mike Flanagan; building steady psychological dread rather than cheap jump scares)."
18. "How will you handle epistolary elements or artifacts? (Excerpts from historical sanity trials, blueprints, diaries, or cassette recordings found in floorboards)."
19. "The Haunting Final Line: What chilling realization or ambiguous image will linger in the reader's mind long after the book is closed?"

## STAGE 7: SYNTHESIS
20. Review all answers. Synthesize them into a masterclass in gothic terror featuring malevolent spatial architecture, psychological unraveling, and relentless atmospheric dread. Present back for approval.
21. Once approved, craft a 12-word pitch and a blood-chilling commercial blurb hook.
