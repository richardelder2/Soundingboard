# Kill Your Darlings Salvage Protocol

The most difficult scenes to cut are not the bad ones—they are the brilliant, beautifully written, atmospheric scenes that do not advance narrative causality or alter character value polarities.

---

## 1. The Psychological Trap: The Sunk-Cost Darling
Authors cling to superfluous scenes because of the emotional energy invested in crafting the prose. When an author is forced to delete a 3,000-word set-piece outright, creative grief often induces writer's block.

---

## 2. The Mechanics of the Salvage Graveyard
Never delete a beloved scene into the void.

1. **The Graveyard Vault (`inputs/graveyard/`):** Cut the scene completely from the manuscript ledger and deposit it into an immutable graveyard folder with timestamp and tags.
2. **The 10% Essence Extraction:** Extract the single best line of dialogue, sharpest sensory metaphor, or character reveal from the cut scene.
3. **Transplantation:** Graft that extracted 10% essence into a mission-critical obligatory scene later in the act.

---

## 3. The 3-Test Litmus Test for Darlings
Before granting a darling scene immunity:
1. **The Domino Test:** If this scene were completely erased, does any subsequent chapter break or become nonsensical? If the story moves forward unaffected, the scene is an essay, not narrative.
2. **The Value Shift Test:** Does the protagonist enter this scene in one emotional/strategic state and exit in its direct opposite?
3. **The Motive Test:** Did the scene happen because a character made an urgent choice, or because the author wanted to show off the world?
