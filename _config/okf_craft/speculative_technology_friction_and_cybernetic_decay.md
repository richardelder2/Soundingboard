# Speculative Technology Friction & Cybernetic Decay

In high-concept science fiction (*Neuromancer*, *Blade Runner*, *Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?*, *The Expanse*), advanced technology is never clean, seamless, or miraculous.

**Authentic speculative technology is gritty, expensive, corporatized, and prone to catastrophic failure.**

---

## 1. The Four Vectors of Hardware Friction

1. **Physical & Thermal Degradation:**
   - Overheating micro-reactors, burned-out optical servos, leaking coolant lines, corroded neural contacts. High-tier gear requires constant maintenance and black-market replacement parts.
2. **Proprietary DRM & Corporate Subscription Traps:**
   - The protagonist's bionic eye requires monthly subscription authentication from an orbital megacorporation. Miss a payment, and resolution drops to monochrome or blinding corporate banner ads obscure their peripheral vision.
3. **Biological Rejection & Somatic Cost:**
   - The human body fights titanium and neural cyberware. Synthetic rejection syndrome requires expensive immunosuppressants, chronic nerve pain, and Phantom Limb feedback.
4. **Planned Obsolescence & Supply Cartels:**
   - The military firmware update bricks legacy neural implants; street street-docs must solder illegal jumper wires to bypass security protocols.

---

## 2. Sensory Anchoring for Tech in Prose

- **Smell & Heat:** The ozone smell of frying solder, the hot chemical stench of burning synthetic fluid, the humid air of a server trench.
- **Acoustic Tells:** The whining high-pitched hum of a cycling capacitor, the dull metallic clicking of a degraded prosthetic finger joint.
- **Tactile Weight:** Cold metal against bare flesh, the grease-stained collar of a cyber-mechanic's jacket, the persistent vibration of a worn cooling fan in a cranial implant.

---

## 3. Collaborative Diagnostic Pass (Tech Friction)

When speculative technology feels too easy or miraculous:
- **Option A (Introduce Supply Scarcity):** Force the protagonist to choose between buying medicine or purchasing replacement battery cells.
- **Option B (Firmware Lockout):** Have corporate security remotely revoke the license for a character's cyberware weapon during a crisis.
- **Option C (Thermal Limit):** Give the character's signature piece of technology a strict operational deadline before it overheats and burns their skin.
