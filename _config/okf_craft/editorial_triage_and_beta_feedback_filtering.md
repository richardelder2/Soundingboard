# Editorial Triage: Filtering Beta Feedback & Navigating Conflict

Writers are routinely paralyzed by contradictory reader feedback. Reader A loves the romance but hates the pacing; Reader B finds the romance distracting and demands more lore. Without a triage filter, manuscripts are edited into homogenized blandness.

---

## 1. The Golden Rule of Reader Diagnostics (Neil Gaiman's Heuristic)
> *"When people tell you something's wrong or doesn't work for them, they are almost always right. When they tell you exactly what they think is wrong and how to fix it, they are almost always wrong."*

Never adopt a reader's proposed prescriptive solution directly. Instead, identify the underlying symptom that triggered their confusion, boredom, or resentment.

---

## 2. The 4-Tier Feedback Classification Grid

| Feedback Type | Symptom | Root Cause | Architectural Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **"I got bored in Ch 6"** | Pacing dip | Lack of causality or missing scene stakes | Cut connective scenes; begin *in media res* with an active decision. |
| **"Character X is annoying"** | Reader repulsion | High competence gap or unearned cynicism | Add an early vulnerability moment or an act of costly loyalty. |
| **"The twist felt cheap"** | Reader betrayal | Missing fair-play clue planting | Retrofit 3 sensory foreshadowing breadcrumbs in Act 1. |
| **"I didn't understand the magic"** | Cognitive friction | Abstract mechanics or rule contradiction | Ground the mechanic in visceral physical consequences and limitations. |

---

## 3. The 3-Vote Rule
Do not touch a single line of prose based on a single reader's complaint. If two independent readers stumble on the exact same beat, flag it for inspection. If three readers stumble on it, the structural problem is confirmed and must be addressed.
