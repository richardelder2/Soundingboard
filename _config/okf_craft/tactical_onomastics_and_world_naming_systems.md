# Tactical Onomastics: World Naming Logic & Anti-Slop Discipline

In fiction, a character or location name is never merely a neutral label. It is the single most frequently repeated acoustic signal on the page. Synthetic AI prose constantly succumbs to "token-ether naming"—plucking generic, frictionless, euphonic fantasy archetypes (*Lyra, Elara, Kaelen, Silas, Rowan, Shadowblade, Thorne, Vance*). 

Master novelists (Tolkien, Ursula K. Le Guin, Gene Wolfe, George R.R. Martin, Frank Herbert, Cormac McCarthy) design names as **sociolinguistic artifacts** governed by phonotactics, caste friction, living speech erosion, and toponymic sediment.

---

## 1. Acoustic Geometry & The Bouba/Kiki Principle

Human brains instinctively map phonetics to physical and psychological realities:
- **Kiki Phonetics (Sharp, Threatening, Angular, Agile):** Unvoiced plosives (/k/, /t/, /p/), affricates (/ch/), and sharp alveolar sibilants (/s/, /z/). Names like *Krag, Vex, Tadhg, Decimus, Drake*. Excellent for military regimes, industrial castes, flinty pragmatists, or jagged landscapes.
- **Bouba Phonetics (Soft, Heavy, Fluid, Ancient, Comforting):** Voiced bilabials (/b/, /m/), liquids (/l/, /r/), nasals (/n/), and rounded open vowels (/o/, /u/). Names like *Halvar, Morwen, Solis, Bror, Oisin*. Excellent for ancient landowning clans, maritime folk, or gentle giants.

**Anti-AI Rule:** AI systems default uniformly to smooth Bouba-Kiki hybrids that roll harmlessly off the tongue (*Aiden, Kaelen, Silas*). Human history is full of ugly, harsh, asymmetric names (*Gudrun, Dunstan, Swithun, Skovgaard, Flavia*). Emphasize acoustic friction.

---

## 2. Sociolinguistic Stratification & Historical Sediment

Great fiction reflects how power, history, and social class structure language:
1. **The Aristocratic Hoarders:** Nobility maintain archaic, polysyllabic, foreign-court, or territorial names (*Valerius of House Caradell, Constance de Malcor*). They preserve ancient spelling to defend their inheritance.
2. **The Guild & Vocational Core:** Artisans, engineers, and clerks possess names tied to sworn crafts, functional tools, or bureaucratic logs (*Wulfric Shipwright, Brother Scriptor-12, Linus Cogswell*).
3. **The Agrarian & Labor Core:** Common folk have blunt, monosyllabic, earthy names with eroded patronymics or physical landmarks (*Halvar Torstein-son, Dunstan Lowford, Bror Claypole*).
4. **The Outcasts & Denied:** Slaves, street gangs, and outlaws are legally denied patronymics. They operate under functional survival monikers or physical defect tags (*Raze, Patch, Two-Tone, Null*).

---

## 3. Toponymy: Landscape Erosion vs. "Fantasy Poetry"

Amateur and synthetic worldbuilders name locations with purple compound clichés (*Whispering Woods, Dragon's Peak, Sunspire, Shadowfen*). Real geography is not poetic; it is **eroded historical description**:
- **The Toponymic Equation:** `[Specific Descriptor or Clan Founder] + [Generic Landscape Feature]`
- **Living Erosion:** Over centuries of slur and rapid speech, compound words fuse and erode:
  - *Oxen-ford* ➔ **Oxford**
  - *Dun-holm* (Hill Island) ➔ **Durham**
  - *Beorma-ing-ham* (Homestead of Beorma's Clan) ➔ **Birmingham**
  - *Black-tarn* ➔ **Blatarn**
  - *Hollow-well* ➔ **Hallowell** ➔ **Holl**
- **The Conquered Substrate Rule:** Invaders often mistake native words for proper names (e.g. *The River Avon* literally means *The River River*, because *afon* was the Celtic word for river). Layer ancient conquered languages beneath modern imperial place names.

---

## 4. The Cast Collision Matrix

A primary cause of reader fatigue and disorientation is phonetically clustered cast rosters:
- **The Initial Collision Trap:** Having *Rob, Ron, Roger, and Richard* in the same story.
- **The Ending Cadence Trap:** Having *Elena, Serena, Rowena, and Althea* in the same scene.

### The 4-Point Differentiation Checklist:
1. **Distinct Initial Letters:** Major cast members must start with distinct phonemes.
2. **Varying Syllabic Meter:** Pair a monosyllabic protagonist (*Bror*) with a dactylic antagonist (*Valerius*) and an iambic ally (*Ronan*).
3. **Different Terminal Codas:** Ensure one name ends in a stop (*-k*), one in an open vowel (*-a*), one in a nasal (*-n*), and one in a sibilant (*-s*).
4. **The Dialogue Stress Test:** If a commander screams the name across an explosion, does it cut through the din? If a lover whispers it in the dark, does it feel intimate or clumsy?

---

## 5. Nickname Drift & The Living Breath Test

A name written on an author's planning document is frozen. On the page, characters shorten, bastardize, weaponize, and soften names:
- **Weaponized Diminutive:** An arrogant rival calling a powerful warrior *"Little Hal"* or *"Tor-boy"*.
- **Emergency Contraction:** Screaming *"Tor!"* instead of *"Torstein!"* under collapse.
- **Intimate Softening:** A mother using an archaic childhood diminutive (*"Siri"* for *Sigrid*).
- If a name cannot survive being degraded, mangled, or affectionately trimmed by other characters in dialogue, it belongs in a museum, not a living novel.
