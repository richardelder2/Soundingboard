# Structural Blueprint Rosetta Stone: Translating Beat Models

Every major story structure model—whether Blake Snyder's *Save the Cat!*, Dan Wells' *7-Point Story Structure*, Dan Harmon's *Story Circle*, Joseph Campbell's *Hero's Journey*, or Randy Ingermanson's *Snowflake Method*—is describing the **exact same fundamental human psychological journey of transformation through crisis**.

In Soundingboard, the author chooses their preferred architectural language. The system never forces an author into a framework they dislike; it uses this Rosetta Stone to translate seamlessly between them.

---

## 2. Choosing Your Authorial Weapon

- **Use Save the Cat! if:** You think in cinematic pacing, commercial screenwriting flow, and act percentages.
- **Use Dan Wells 7-Point if:** You are a plot-first or mystery writer who prefers **working backward from the Climax** (Resolution ➔ Hook ➔ Midpoint ➔ Turns ➔ Pinches).
- **Use Harmon's Story Circle if:** You focus on psychological character loops (Comfort ➔ Need ➔ Go ➔ Search ➔ Find ➔ Take ➔ Return ➔ Change).
- **Use the Snowflake Method if:** You are a progressive fractal planner who starts with one sentence and expands outward.

## 3. Collaborative Diagnostic Pass (The Translation Pass)

When an author feels stuck using one model:
- **Option A (Translate to 7-Point):** *"If your Midpoint is the secret heist success, your Pinch 1 must be the syndicate realizing they were robbed and locking the city gates."*
- **Option B (Translate to Story Circle):** *"You have the 'Find', but your protagonist hasn't paid the 'Take' price yet. What does seizing this prize cost them emotionally?"*
- **Option C (Retain Author Dialect):** Map the author's custom scene sequence directly onto Stage 02 planning cards without renaming their beats.
