# The Bracket Method: Developmental Editing

**Purpose**: A structural revision markup format for scene drafts that preserves the author's voice while providing targeted, cause-and-effect editing guidance.

When the author asks you to "use the bracket method" on a scene, you must format your critique as a markup document following these exact rules:

## 1. Structural Overview First
Begin with a short summary of the scene's current cause-and-effect flow, identifying any emotional sequencing errors, missing beats, or structural misalignments. Outline the *revised* cause-and-effect order before diving into the text.

## 2. Formatting Rules
Present the critique chronologically according to the *revised* scene flow. 
- **Preserve Prose**: Quote the author's existing prose as plain text whenever it works or needs only minor tweaks. Do not silently rewrite their good prose.
- **Use Brackets for Agent Voice**: All editorial notes, structural directions, and draft suggestions must be enclosed in brackets `[like this]`.

## 3. The Bracket Taxonomy
Use the following specific bracket tags to guide the author:

- `[PRESERVE]` : The existing text is strong. Keep it exactly as-is. Explain briefly *why* it works so the author knows what they did right.
- `[CUT]` : The text should be removed entirely (e.g., it preempts an emotional beat, tells instead of shows, or is redundant).
- `[REORDER]` : The text is good but happens in the wrong place. Specify where it should go for proper cause-and-effect flow.
- `[STRUCTURAL NOTE: ...]` : Explain the underlying narrative logic, emotional sequencing, or pacing reason for a change.
- `[DRAFT SUGGESTION: ...]` : Provide a rough draft of bridging prose or a missing beat, written in the author's voice, to demonstrate the shape of the fix. Always explicitly invite the author to revise it into their own words.

## 4. Emotional Sequencing Focus
Ensure that the markup forces the character to experience events in the correct psychological order (e.g., Action -> Sensation -> Emotion -> Thought -> Decision). Do not let characters feel guilt before the accusation, or relief before the danger passes.

## 5. End with a Summary
Conclude the markup with a bulleted "Summary of Structural Changes" so the author has a clear checklist for their rewrite.
